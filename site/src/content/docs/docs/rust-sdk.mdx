Two crates hold notes for a Rust program:

- `worgl-client` talks to a server. `Client` makes one typed call per
  route, and `Purse` runs a holder over HTTP and saves it after every change.
  Most programs need only this.
- `worgl-sdk` is the holder's side with no networking and no files: a note
  store that requests, pays, receives and refreshes notes while keeping the
  privacy rules. It runs anywhere, WebAssembly included.

## Add them

```sh
cargo add worgl worgl-sdk worgl-client
cargo add tokio --features macros,rt-multi-thread
```

`worgl-client` runs its requests on Tokio, over rustls with the platform's
certificate roots. `worgl` brings the shared types, such as `PublicKey` for a
mint's key and `Note`.

## A payment with a purse

<Code code={purseExample} lang="rust" />

This is the example in `worgl-client`'s crate documentation, which CI
compiles. A `Purse` wraps a holder, a `Client` and a `Store`. It reads mint
info by itself, submits each transfer at its time, retries what fails on the
way (a resent transfer gets the same answer), and saves after every change,
before handing out anything that depends on it. After a `busy` answer it
waits as long as the mint's `Retry-After` asks, up to 30 seconds, before the
next try.

Before it keeps an answer, the purse checks it against the keys of the
transfer's mint, read after the answer: one note for each of the transfer's
outputs, with that output's amount and key, signed by the mint. It keeps
none of an answer that is not the mint's, such as a forged note from a
server that is not the mint's, nor of one whose keys it cannot read. The
transfer is listed in `Submitted::refused`, with `Error::Answer` when the
answer is not the mint's and the outcome `Kept`, and one the purse made
stays outgoing, for `settle` to send again.

`Purse::open` keeps the purse in a JSON file. The file holds every note's
secret key, and the notes are bearer, so keep it private and encrypted at
rest. Implement `Store` to keep it elsewhere; the `worgl` command keeps its
purse encrypted under a passphrase this way, with `worgl_sdk::vault`.
Several processes can share the file: before each change a purse locks it
and reads it again, so none saves over another's change. A `Store` of your
own does the same by implementing `lock`.

The mint's answers to a payment hold notes for both sides, and
`Submitted::theirs` lists the other side's. The payee hands them back to the
payer: they are the payer's change, which `Purse::accept` checks against the
mint's keys, keeps, and uses to settle the payer's transfer. When the payer
submits instead, with `Purse::submit`, it hands `theirs` to the payee, who
keeps its notes with `Purse::accept` the same way. The payer's purse keeps
them for the payee too, until the payer calls `Purse::forget_owed` with
them once they are handed over, as `worgl submit` does.
`Purse::take_from_faucet` asks a playground [faucet](../running-a-faucet/)
for a note, to a fresh key like any other.

## What else a purse does

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `request(&mint, amount)` | Asks to be paid: a `PaymentRequest` of fresh keys to send the payer. |
| `request_at_most(&mint, amount, largest)` | The same, in notes no larger than `largest`. When `pay` refuses a request as `PayError::Fragmented`, a request in notes no larger than its `at_most` is one the payer can pay. |
| `pay(&request, spacing)` | Answers a request with a `Payment` of signed transfers. Refuses, before signing, a request for a note the mint does not make or to a key the purse has paid already. |
| `receive(&request, &payment)` | Checks a payment, submits each transfer at its time and keeps the notes. |
| `settle(spacing)` | Sends the transfers not yet seen answered again, and takes in the change. |
| `cancel(&transfer)` / `forget(&transfer)` | Ends an outgoing transfer: `cancel` takes back the note of one the mint never applied, `forget` drops one whose note is gone. Both keep its change keys, so change handed over later is still taken in. |
| `holder().payments()` / `forget_owed(&notes)` | The payments the purse made and keeps, each with the `Payment` to send again (less any transfer cancelled or forgotten) and the payee's notes from every answer the purse got itself, by settling, refreshing or submitting; then stops keeping those notes once they are handed over. |
| `submit(&transfers)` / `accept(&notes)` | One side submits; the other keeps what it is handed, and settles the transfers it answers. |
| `withdraw(&request)` | Stops expecting the notes of a request that will not be paid. |
| `balance(&mint)` | What the purse has at a mint, in parts that do not overlap: held now (spendable, frozen, unknown), sending to payees, change and refreshing on their way back, and requested. `Balance::total` adds what it holds and what comes back, counting no note twice. Unconfirmed change of dropped or cancelled transfers is in no part: after a cancel it comes only if the mint spent the note taken back. |
| `refresh(&mint)` | Pays the notes that die at the next rotation back to the purse, each at a random moment before it by the server's clock, and resends the transfers not yet seen answered that must land before it. Returns once the last is done, which may be most of a period away. |
| `plan_refresh(&mint)` | The same plan, for an application with its own scheduler to `submit`. |
| `prune(&mint)` | Drops notes whose layer the mint's info says it has dropped, and returns the value lost. This machine's clock plays no part. |

A resent transfer gets the same notes only while the mint keeps its answer
(see [Error codes](../error-codes/)), and at the latest until those notes
expire. After that, a transfer already applied is refused as `spent` or
`expired`, and `Refused::at_stake` says it was possibly applied and what may
be lost: the change can then only come from the counterparty, for
`accept`. The purse drops such a transfer, keeping its change keys. It
cancels one refused as `layer_full`, which the mint never applied and never
will, and one refused as `policy` that the policy the mint publishes refuses
too, taking its note back; `Refused::outcome` says what it did
(see [Error codes](../error-codes/#sending-a-request-again)). `refresh`
resends the transfers whose spent note dies at the next rotation, or died at
the last one. The answer to a payment's transfer keeps the payee's notes, in
`MadePayment::owed`, for the payer to hand over: the purse cannot tell
whether the payee submitted the payment or the resend carried it out.

`Client` errors keep the mint's stable code, such as `spent` or `frozen`, in
`Error::code`, `Error::is_transient` says whether trying again later may
work, and `Error::retry_after` how long a `busy` mint asked to wait first.
`is_transient` counts `layer_full` out: a transfer refused so is refused
every time, though a mint request may pass after the next rotation. See
[Error codes](../error-codes/).

`Spacing` sets the seconds between transfers that belong together, as a
range the purse picks from at random. `Spacing::default()` spreads them; a
closer spacing lets the mint guess the notes had one owner. See the
[Privacy model](../privacy/).

## The holder on its own

<Code code={holderExample} lang="rust" />

This example, from `worgl-sdk`'s crate documentation, runs a mint in memory
in place of the server. CI runs it.

1. The payee calls `Holder::request` and sends the `PaymentRequest`.
2. The payer calls `Holder::pay` and returns the `Payment`.
3. The payee checks it with `PaymentRequest::check`, submits each transfer at
   its time, records each answer with `Holder::settle`, which checks it
   against the mint's keys and takes the new notes in, and hands the rest of
   the answers, `Holder::theirs`, back to the payer: its change.
4. The payer checks their signatures and takes them in with
   `Holder::take_answers`, which settles the transfers they answer. Without
   them, it resends its outgoing transfers at the times `Holder::resend`
   gives and records each answer with `Holder::settle`, which works while
   the mint keeps its answers, and keeps the payee's notes from it in
   `Holder::payments`, in case the payee has not submitted.

A `MintView`, read from a mint's info, tells the holder which notes can move
now, which are frozen until the next rotation and which die at it. It holds
the info's layers, which no clock moves, so read the info again after a
rotation. With a schedule in the info, `MintView::spendable_at` and
`MintView::expires_at` say when a frozen note can move and when a note dies
untouched, and `Holder::first_expiry` when the first of the holder's would,
counting the notes its outgoing transfers spend: the time to refresh by.
`Holder::refresh` keeps notes alive by paying the ones about to
expire to the holder itself, and `Holder::due_resends` gives the outgoing
transfers to resend before the next rotation. Both spread them over the time
left from a `now` by the mint's clock, which `Client::view_and_time` reads
from the `Date` of the server's answer, and plan them all at once when that
time has passed the rotation's. The holder saves to JSON with its secret keys.

`Holder::merge` takes another copy of a holder's purse into it, such as a
backup or another device's copy, and returns what changed, `Merged`: what it
took in, the notes the other copy spent, the transfers it settled,
cancelled or forgot, and the transfers that spend a note both copies spent.
What only the other copy had may be out of date, so it comes in unchecked,
`Holder::unchecked`, and counts in `Balance::unchecked` until the mint says
otherwise; `Holder::keep` and `Holder::discard` take its answers. Its
documentation weighs what asking the mint shows. `Holder::join` takes in a
part as current, such as the holder of another server's mints for one
backup of both. With the `vault` feature,
`worgl_sdk::vault` seals a holder under a passphrase into the
[purse file](../command-line/#the-purse-file) `worgl` keeps, with the server
of each mint beside it, and opens one, a backup of the TypeScript SDK's
included.
